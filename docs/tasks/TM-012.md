# TM-012: Operator approves or refuses an order

## Goal

The operator approves an order awaiting approval, optionally changing its limit price, or refuses it with an optional note. A refusal ends the trade signal with `SignalDeclined` and `RefusedByOperator`.

## Scope

In scope:

- Approve and refuse application commands, the order's domain transitions, and the screen actions on the orders screen from TM-011.
- Orders in other statuses on the orders screen and the start page from TM-011, starting with approved and refused orders.
- The orchestrator step that records the signal outcome after the order reaches a terminal status.

Out of scope:

- Submission (TM-014).
- Lapse at the end of signal validity (TM-017).
- Cancellation (TM-019).

## Requirements

### Approve

- An order awaiting approval becomes approved.
- An optional new limit price is Money greater than zero, in the order's currency, with at most two decimal places. Other input is refused.
- With a new limit price, the quantity is recalculated from the snapshot's order amount: the largest whole number of units whose cost does not exceed it.
- If the recalculated quantity is zero, the approval is refused with a validation message and the order stays awaiting approval.
- No other bound applies to the new limit price.
- Approval is refused when signal validity has already ended (now ≥ `validUntil`). The lapse itself is TM-017.

### Refuse

- An order awaiting approval becomes refused, a terminal status.
- The optional note is stored only in the audit trail and never in the signal outcome.
- The trade signal is declined with `RefusedByOperator`, and `SignalDeclined` is recorded in the outbox.

### Common rules

- The operator cannot change the instrument, the order amount, or the quantity directly.
- The aggregate owns the transitions: approve and refuse are allowed only from awaiting approval.
- Concurrent decisions, such as a double submit or two tabs, are resolved by an optimistic version check: one succeeds and the other is refused.
- The signal outcome is recorded in a separate transaction after the order reaches its terminal status, coordinated by the orchestrator, which resumes after a crash between the two.
- The audit records the operator, the time, the action, the old and new limit price and quantity, and the note.
- Screen actions use CSRF protection and are available only to the authenticated operator.

### Orders in other statuses

- The orders screen lists orders in other statuses after the orders awaiting approval, through the read model from TM-009 extended for them ([ADR-0004](../ADR/adr-0004-livewire-eloquent-read-models.md)).
- Each shows its status and execution progress: none, partial, or complete, with the executed quantity.
- Non-terminal orders are listed in full. Terminal orders are paginated, most recent first.
- The start page also shows the number of non-terminal orders not awaiting approval.
- Statuses added by later tasks (submitting, open, cancelling, and the other terminal ones) appear as those tasks introduce them.

### Automatic updates

The orders screen updates itself (TM-011). An update keeps an open approve or refuse form and what the operator typed, while the list around the form keeps updating.

A decision refused by the version check says that the order was already decided and that nothing changed. An approval refused because signal validity ended says so.

## Decisions

- The owner decided on 2026-10-31 to refuse an approval whose recalculated quantity is zero.
- The refusal note stays in the audit trail and is not reported to the analytics application.
- The owner decided on 2026-11-03 that automatic updates keep an open form and what the operator typed, while the list around the form keeps updating.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Behat: approving without a change makes the order approved with its original limit price and quantity.
- [ ] Behat: approving with a new limit price recalculates the quantity from the snapshot's order amount. Example: an order amount of 1000 EUR at 10.50 EUR gives a quantity of 95.
- [ ] Behat: approving with a limit price that leaves zero quantity is refused, and the order stays awaiting approval.
- [ ] Behat: an operator refusal ends with `SignalDeclined` and `RefusedByOperator`, the note appears in the audit trail, and the note is not in the outcome.
- [ ] Domain unit tests:
  - a new limit price of 0.00 EUR, a negative one, one in another currency, and one with three decimal places (10.505 EUR) are refused, and 0.01 EUR is accepted;
  - allowed and refused transitions;
  - quantity recalculation;
  - the refusal after signal validity ends;
  - immutability.
- [ ] PostgreSQL test: a stale version makes the second of two concurrent decisions fail without changes.
- [ ] Feature tests: each screen action invokes the intended application command and requires authentication and CSRF.
- [ ] Feature test: the approve action accepts no instrument, order amount, or quantity, and a request that sends them leaves those values unchanged.
- [ ] Feature test: an approved order is listed with its status and execution progress none, and the start page counts it among the non-terminal orders not awaiting approval.
- [ ] Feature test: refused orders are listed as terminal and paginated, most recent first.
- [ ] Feature test: after an order is approved, the next render of the orders screen shows its new status and a new last update time.
- [ ] Feature test: an update while an approve or refuse form is open keeps the entered limit price or note.
- [ ] Feature test: a decision refused by the version check, and an approval refused after signal validity ended, each show their message.
- [ ] A manual check in the local environment is described in the PR: on the orders screen, the operator approves one order and refuses another, and `SignalDeclined` with `RefusedByOperator` appears in the demo analytics application's outcome queue (TM-006).
- [ ] A manual check in the local environment is described in the PR: with an approve form open and a new limit price typed, the order from a new trade signal appears in the list, and the form keeps the typed value.
- [ ] `CI_WITH_MUTATION=diff make ci` passes.
