# TM-009: Order creation awaiting approval

## Goal

A trade signal that passes evaluation leads to exactly one order, awaiting approval, priced and sized from the investment configuration. A trade signal whose order amount cannot buy one unit is declined with `OrderAmountTooSmall`.

## Scope

In scope:

- The `Order` aggregate, its repository, persistence and migration.
- The order creation step of the orchestrator.
- The read-only Eloquent read model for orders awaiting approval, and the Deptrac rules for read models ([ADR-0004](../ADR/adr-0004-livewire-eloquent-read-models.md)).

Out of scope:

- The GUI (TM-011).
- Approval and refusal (TM-012).

## Requirements

### Pricing and sizing

- Limit price = reference price × (1 + limit price tolerance), rounded down to the currency's minor unit (0.01 EUR).
- Quantity = the largest whole number of units whose cost at the limit price does not exceed the order amount. There are no fractional units, and commissions are not included.
- A quantity of zero means no order: the trade signal ends with `SignalDeclined` and `OrderAmountTooSmall`.

Worked examples:

| Reference price | Tolerance | Unrounded | Limit price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10.00 EUR | 2 % | 10.20 | 10.20 EUR |
| 12.345 EUR | 1.5 % | 12.530175 | 12.53 EUR |

- At a limit price of 10.20 EUR, an order amount of 1000 EUR gives a quantity of 98, costing 999.60 EUR.
- At a limit price of 10.20 EUR, an order amount of 1020.00 EUR gives a quantity of 100, and 1019.99 EUR gives 99.
- An order amount of 1000 EUR gives a quantity of 1 at a limit price of 1000.00 EUR, and a quantity of 0 at 1000.01 EUR.

### Creating the order

The order is created with:

- status awaiting approval and execution progress none;
- direction buy and the instrument (symbol and MIC);
- the limit price and quantity;
- a snapshot of the settings it used: order amount, limit price tolerance, currency, and brokerage account;
- references to the trade signal identity and the investment configuration identity.

Further rules:

- Trade Maneuver generates the order identity. TM-014 derives the `clientOrderId` from it.
- A trade signal leads to at most one order, enforced by a database uniqueness constraint.
- The order is created in its own transaction after evaluation. The orchestrator resumes creation after a crash, and the uniqueness constraint keeps it idempotent.
- Order creation is audited with the snapshot.
- Another trade signal for an instrument already bought leads to another order. There is no position tracking.

### Read side

The read model for orders awaiting approval provides:

- for the order: identity, status, limit price, quantity, order amount, and execution progress;
- for its trade signal: instrument (symbol and MIC), direction, confidence, horizon, expected move, reference price with currency and `asOf`, `validUntil`, and the analytics application.

Orders are sorted by `validUntil`, soonest first. The read model loads no aggregates and rejects writes.

## Decisions

- The owner decided on 2026-10-31 to round the limit price down to 0.01 EUR. Tick sizes are not handled in this iteration. A broker rejection caused by the tick size ends as `OrderRejected` (TM-015).

## Constraints

- Exact arithmetic follows the Money ADR from TM-003. No `float`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Behat: a valid `long` trade signal creates one order awaiting approval with the expected limit price, quantity, and snapshot, visible through the awaiting-approval read model.
- [ ] Behat: a trade signal whose quantity rounds down to zero ends with `SignalDeclined` and `OrderAmountTooSmall`, and no order is created.
- [ ] Behat: a second trade signal for the same instrument creates a second order.
- [ ] Domain unit tests:
  - limit price rounding, including the worked examples and tolerance 0 %;
  - quantity boundaries: the worked examples for an exact multiple and one cent below it;
  - the zero-quantity decline, using the worked example;
  - snapshot immutability;
  - reconstitution without domain events.
- [ ] PostgreSQL test with independent connections: concurrent order creation for one trade signal leaves exactly one order.
- [ ] Mapping test: an order round trip preserves exact amounts and the snapshot.
- [ ] PostgreSQL test: saving or deleting a read model instance, or writing through its query builder, throws an exception and changes nothing.
- [ ] Deptrac allows `Ui/Livewire` and query adapters to depend on read models. It fails for any other `Ui` dependency on `Infrastructure`, and for a dependency on a read model from `Domain`, `Application`, or a repository. The PR shows the output of temporary violating classes, which are not committed.
- [ ] A manual check in the local environment is described in the PR, using the demo analytics application (TM-006) and the simulated broker (TM-008):
  - a trade signal for `DEMO_A` creates an order awaiting approval;
  - a trade signal for `DEMO_A` with a reference price above the order amount ends with `SignalDeclined` and `OrderAmountTooSmall` in the outcome queue.
- [ ] `CI_WITH_MUTATION=diff make ci` passes, and `make ci` shows no unexplained surviving mutants in pricing and sizing.
